Feasibility

Can Your Tattoo Be Covered? (Honest Answer)

Most tattoos can be covered. What decides it is how well-put the old tattoo is and what kind of ink was used. Age and fading help: a tattoo that's softened over the years gives a new design more to work with than one still solid and fresh. Heavy blackwork is some of the hardest to bury: nothing lighter than the old black will hide it, so the new design has to work at that value or darker. Fresh, heavily saturated color is its own challenge, because there's less room underneath for a new palette to sit on. Size matters too: a cover-up design typically needs to run about two to three times the size of the original to fully absorb it, so a small tattoo in a tight spot has fewer directions to grow into.

When an old tattoo is heavily saturated, a few sessions of laser lightening beforehand can open up design options that wouldn't otherwise be on the table. I'll tell you honestly if that's worth considering for your piece. And sometimes, even with lightening, the only way to truly bury what's there is to go really dark with the new design. If that's true for your tattoo, I say so before we start, not after.

The Approach

My Approach: Why Bio-Organic Design Works for Cover-Ups

My signature style for cover-ups is bio-organic: flowing organic forms instead of rigid geometric shapes, which is exactly what old ink needs on top of it. A hard-edged shape has to fight the linework that's already there; it either lines up with the old tattoo or it doesn't. Bio-organic doesn't run into that problem. The forms curve and shift, the values run deep, and the line density changes across the piece the way it would in real bone or tissue, so the design has places to absorb old ink into instead of fighting it. The deep, saturated shading is doing real work too: it runs as dark or darker than the old ink, so the two blend instead of one showing through the other.

Bio-organic isn't the only style that covers well. Traditional Japanese works from the opposite direction: bold coverage, heavy black, dynamic backgrounds built to fill space and carry visual weight over whatever's underneath. Floral covers a different way again: dense, layered petals stack shadow over shadow, and a flower's natural, irregular edge hides the boundary of the old tattoo better than a hard-edged design could. Which style fits depends on what's already on your skin, and that's a conversation we have at the consultation, not a decision I make for you.

How the Cover-Up Process Works

1

Free consultation

Bring clear photos of the existing tattoo, or send them ahead if you're consulting remotely. We talk through what you want instead, what style direction fits, and whether it's a full cover-up or a rework. The consultation is free, in person at the studio or remote if you're traveling in.

3

Custom design

The new design gets built around the placement, flow and density of what's already there, not stamped on top of it. Much of it gets drawn directly on your skin the day of the session. I don't send a finished design out before the appointment; some remote consults get a simple sketch first so you know the direction.

4

Sessions & deposit

Plan for a cover-up to run one to two sessions more than the same piece would take on clean skin. I book eight-hour days. A deposit locks your dates: $100 minimum, up to 20% on larger multi-session projects, and it comes off your final price.

5

Healing & the healed result

You'll get full aftercare guidance for healing the new tattoo, same as any piece. The healed piece, not the fresh photo, is the honest test of a cover-up: it's what tells you the old tattoo is really gone.

Questions

Cover-Up FAQs

Can any tattoo be covered up?

Most can. What decides it is how well-put the old tattoo is and what kind of ink was used. Some blacks are harder to cover than others. I try not to turn people away: I look at what's on your skin and tell you honestly what it needs.

What kinds of tattoos can you cover up?

Names, old designs, badly done tattoos: I've covered them all. And when a tattoo doesn't need full covering, a rework can rebuild it instead.

Do cover-up tattoos have to be dark?

Sometimes. Over the years I've learned that some old tattoos can only be fully buried by going really dark. If that's true for your piece, I tell you before we start, not after.

Do cover-ups take more sessions than a fresh tattoo?

Usually one to two sessions more than the same piece on clean skin. The total depends on the size of the cover-up and what we put over it.

What's the difference between a cover-up and a rework?

A cover-up hides the old tattoo completely under a new design. A rework keeps a tattoo you mostly like and rebuilds it: sharpening lines, adding shading or color. I do both.

What's the best style for covering an old tattoo?

Bio-organic is one of the strongest: the deep values and organic flow can absorb almost anything underneath. See my bio-organic page for how that style handles cover-ups.

How much does a cover-up cost?

Every cover-up is scoped individually at the consultation, based on size, detail and how many sessions it needs, so I won't quote a number without seeing your tattoo first. As a rule of thumb, plan for one to two sessions more than the same piece would take on clean skin. A deposit locks your dates: $100 minimum, up to 20% on larger multi-session projects, and it comes off your final price.

Do cover-ups hurt more?

Not really: it's the same process, not a different kind of pain. Skin over old ink can feel somewhat more sensitive than fresh skin, and cover-up sittings often run denser because of the amount of coverage needed. So a session can feel more intense, but there's nothing extra happening to you.

Do I need laser removal first?

Usually no. Most cover-ups work with what's already there. When the old tattoo is heavily saturated, like solid blackwork or dense fresh color, a few sessions of laser lightening first can open up design options that wouldn't otherwise be possible. Whether that's worth it for your piece is something I'll tell you honestly at the consultation, not something I push on everyone.

How big does the new tattoo need to be?

As a rule of thumb, a cover-up design needs to run about two to three times the size of the original tattoo to fully absorb it. That's not a hard rule though: flow and placement matter as much as raw size. A design that follows your body's lines can cover more with less than one that fights the anatomy it's sitting on.

Can you cover scars?

Case by case. A scar has to be fully healed first, typically a year or more, before it's ready to tattoo over. From there, what's possible depends on the scar's texture and how well that particular skin holds ink, which is something I assess in person or from clear photos at a consultation, not something I can answer in general.
